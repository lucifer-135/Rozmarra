Rozmarra: A Digital Twin That Predicts Low and High Blood Sugar During Religious Fasting

Rozmarra helps doctors plan safer fasts for people with Type 2 Diabetes in India.

Team NOT_SOMEONE_IMPORTANT, Indian Institute of Technology (Indian School of Mines) Dhanbad

Shivansh Gautam (team leader), Aman Soni and Divy Sharma

Rozmarra is a research prototype. It is not a certified medical device, and it must not be used to make decisions about real patients.

Many people with diabetes in India fast, often without medical advice

101 million

people in India have diabetes, and 136 million have prediabetes (ICMR-INDIAB, 2023).

49%

of 300 adults with Type 2 Diabetes in North India had fasted in the previous year (2020 study).

1.35%

of them had discussed their diet with a doctor or dietitian before fasting, and only about 4% had checked their glucose during the fast.

Navratri, Karva Chauth, Ekadashi, Ramadan, Jain fasts and Chhath Puja are a part of everyday life for millions of families. Fasting is a personal and religious decision. Rozmarra aims to help the doctor make each fast safer, and it never judges the decision to fast.

Fasting changes the risk of low and high blood sugar

Medicines

Sulfonylureas and insulin keep lowering glucose when a person does not eat for many hours. A slow CYP2C9 gene variant keeps glimepiride in the body for longer.

Long hours without food or water

Some fasts last from sunrise to moonrise, and some do not allow water. People who take SGLT2 inhibitors may become dehydrated.

Large meals at the end

The meal that breaks the fast is often large and sweet, and it can cause high blood sugar in the hours that follow.

The EPIDIAR study of 12,243 people in 13 countries found that severe low blood sugar needing hospital care was 7.5 times more common during Ramadan for people with Type 2 Diabetes than in other months.

Rozmarra | Team NOT_SOMEONE_IMPORTANT3

Doctors have a risk score, but it cannot show when and why the risk changes

What doctors have today

  • The IDF-DAR 2021 risk score places a person into low, moderate or high fasting risk.
  • The IDF-DAR guide asks people to break the fast below 70 mg/dL or above 300 mg/dL, or when they feel unwell.
  • Continuous glucose monitors (CGM) and smartwatches record data, but nothing turns it into advice for a fast.

What is missing

  • The score is calculated once, so it cannot show the hour of highest risk.
  • The doctor cannot test how a lower dose or a new dose time may change the risk.
  • No early warning helps during the fast itself.
  • Patients rarely receive a written plan in their own language.

Rozmarra supports the doctor before, during and after the fast

Before the fast

The doctor tests a fasting plan on the patient's virtual twin and compares two options, such as a lower dose. Rozmarra shows the glucose curve with its uncertainty band.

During the fast

Rozmarra estimates the chance of low blood sugar in the next 60 minutes from the recent CGM readings, the smartwatch data and the twin. Each alert lists three reasons.

After approval

The doctor approves one plan. Rozmarra prints a plan in English or Hindi with the times to check glucose and the rule for breaking the fast.

Rozmarra never changes medicines by itself. The doctor makes and approves every treatment decision.

Rozmarra combines two data streams

The health record

  • Diagnosis, HbA1c, kidney function, weight and blood pressure
  • Medicines with doses and times, and the CYP2C9 genotype
  • The inputs of the IDF-DAR 2021 score
  • Stored as FHIR R4 bundles, and labelled as synthetic

Continuous data

  • CGM readings every 5 or 15 minutes
  • Heart rate, heart rate variability, steps and sleep from a smartwatch
  • Meals, medicine doses and activity in the fasting plan

The digital twin models glucose, insulin, meals and medicines

  • A physiology model describes glucose, insulin action and insulin for one person, minute by minute.
  • Medicine activity curves cover sulfonylureas, premixed and basal insulin, metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors and more.
  • The CYP2C9 genotype slows the clearance of sulfonylureas. For example, the *1/*3 genotype gives 65% of the usual speed.
  • The team fitted one twin to each of 89 real patients from ShanghaiT2DM and CGMacros.
  • 100 parameter samples give a band from the 10th to the 90th percentile.

The prediction model explains each estimate in plain language

  • LightGBM models estimate the chance of low blood sugar (below 70 mg/dL) within the next 60 minutes and the chance of high blood sugar after the fast is broken.
  • The features combine CGM readings, 14 days of CGM history, the twin's forecasts, smartwatch signals, the fasting context and the health record.
  • Isotonic calibration turns scores into estimated chances.
  • A prediction needs a current CGM reading and 70% of the readings of the last 2 hours.

The team tested Rozmarra on real data and on simulated fasting days

Real normal days

Held-out days of 89 real patients, with five-fold cross-validation grouped by person. These days contain no fasts.

Simulated fasting days

500 virtual patients with 1,500 fasting episodes. The models learned from 400 patients and were tested on 100 patients they had never seen.

Real meals

The model for high blood sugar, trained only on simulated fasts, was applied to 244 real meals that came after at least 8 hours without food.

  • The team compared the model with simpler methods: the last glucose value, a straight-line trend, the twin alone, the IDF-DAR score alone and a model that uses only CGM data.
  • The 95% intervals come from bootstrapping by patient.
  • No open dataset contains CGM data from Indian festival fasts, so the fasting results come from simulation.

The patient receives a printed plan in English or Hindi

  • The doctor approves a plan before Rozmarra can print it.
  • The plan shows the rule for breaking the fast near the top: below 70 mg/dL or above 300 mg/dL, or when the patient feels unwell.
  • It lists the times to check glucose, the medicine changes, advice on water and the meal that breaks the fast.
  • Every sentence comes from fixed templates. Rozmarra does not use AI to write medical advice.
  • The Hindi plan uses the patient's own word for the fast and the name in Devanagari.

Safety, ethics, respect for faith and limitations

Safety and ethics

  • Rozmarra supports the doctor. It never changes medicines by itself.
  • It never judges the decision to fast, and it uses each patient's own word for the fast.
  • All patients in the dashboard are synthetic, and the real data comes only from open anonymised datasets.
  • Patient plan text comes only from fixed templates.

Limitations

  • Rozmarra is a research prototype. It is not a certified medical device.
  • No doctor or pharmacist has reviewed the clinical content.
  • The real data comes from China and the United States, not from India.
  • The fasting results come from simulated fasting days.
  • The median warning time of 25 minutes is shorter than the target of 30 minutes.
  • The IDF-DAR score was designed for Ramadan, and Rozmarra applies it to other fasts as an approximation.

Next steps

A clinical study

A study with Indian patients during real festival fasts would test the predictions and the alert levels, with clinicians reviewing the content.

ABDM integration

Health records could come from the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission through FHIR, with the patient's consent.

More fasts and languages

The team would add more regional fasts and plans in more Indian languages, such as Marathi, Bengali and Tamil.

A real product would also need approval as a medical device, for example from the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO).
